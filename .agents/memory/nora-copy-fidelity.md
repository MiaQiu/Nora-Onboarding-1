---
name: Nora onboarding copy fidelity
description: Why the Nora onboarding should preserve supplied copy even when it reads awkwardly.
---

For Nora onboarding, treat the latest user-supplied copy as the editorial source of truth. Keep its sequence and wording, including punctuation and awkward phrases; personalize child-name placeholders without rewriting the surrounding sentence.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested that the content not be changed after an earlier version altered it. An apparent typo is not permission to edit their script.

**How to apply:** When changing onboarding screens, compare rendered text against the current supplied attachment. Ask before correcting wording or adding explanatory copy.

When a replacement script retains final-plan goal and hard-moment placeholders but omits the questions collecting them, retain the previous goal and hard-moment questions.

**Why:** The user explicitly chose to keep those questions rather than remove the two summary lines.

**How to apply:** Keep those answers grounded in parent selections; do not infer them from the agreement statements or invent plan values.

The retained hard-moment questions include “What's hardest about screen time right now?” as well as the separate “When it's time to stop, what usually happens” question.

**Why:** The user pointed out the hard-moment question was missing when only the stopping-behavior question had been retained.

**How to apply:** Do not treat these two questions as interchangeable during future flow replacements.
